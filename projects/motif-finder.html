Genomic pattern recognition tool.

Can a simple, general tool find promoters, enhancers, and silencers in any organism's DNA and tell real signals from chance matches?

Started as a team project, led by Jade Cruz, at UC Santa Cruz.

The problem

Short regulatory motifs match by chance all over a genome. A tool that only lists matches can mislead, so it needs to say which matches stand out from the background.

The data

A synthetic test set with motifs planted at known positions, and the 154,478 base pair Arabidopsis thaliana chloroplast genome (GenBank NC_000932) as a real test.

Methods

The tool scans deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) sequences in FASTA files on both strands, using a built-in motif library, custom motifs in International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry (IUPAC) notation, and position weight matrices. Enrichment is tested against sequences generated from a first-order Markov model that keeps base and dinucleotide composition.

Findings

All 10 planted sites were recovered at the exact positions and strands. In the chloroplast genome, every bacterial-style promoter element was more common upstream of real genes than in random windows; the −35 box was 2.5 times more common (p = .004). The scan found the promoter of psbA, one of the best-characterized chloroplast promoters.

FOR THE TECHNICAL READER

Reverse-strand matches are reported in forward-strand coordinates, palindromic sites are counted once, and overlapping matches are kept. Outputs include every hit, observed-versus-expected counts with p-values, and a motif map figure. Unit tests cover the core functions.
