Renewable energy permits and carbon emissions.

How did residential renewable energy installations change in Sacramento and Rancho Cordova from 2020 to 2024, and what does that mean for carbon dioxide emissions?

Research Analyst, California Homebuilding Foundation.

The problem

Local governments track renewable energy adoption to plan for emissions targets. Permit data show what is actually being built, and which parts of the transition are stalling.

The data

Construction Industry Research Board (CIRB) residential building permits for Sacramento and Rancho Cordova, 2020 to 2024.

Methods

Permit counts were summarized by year and type. Carbon dioxide (CO2) effects were recalculated under low, central, and high assumptions, with every assumption listed in the code.

Findings

Electric vehicle (EV) charger permits grew sharply (Sacramento: 12 to 819), while solar photovoltaic installations fell 17% in Sacramento and 85% in Rancho Cordova. Under central assumptions, new chargers avoid about 3,362 tonnes of CO2 per year, while fewer solar installations lose about 738 tonnes of offset per year.

FOR THE TECHNICAL READER

The largest uncertainty is how many gasoline cars each new charger permit replaces (0.5 to 3), which drives the wide low-to-high range (1,405 to 10,223 tonnes per year). Raw CIRB data are not redistributed.
