GLP-1 medication use and mental health.

How common is glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) medication use among U.S. adults with diabetes, and is it associated with depression or anxiety?

Student analyst with Dennis R. Trinidad, PhD, MPH, UC San Diego Herbert Wertheim School of Public Health.

The problem

GLP-1 medications are now widely used for diabetes and weight loss. Whether use differs by mental health history, and whether mental health explains differences in who uses these drugs, matters for equitable access.

The data

The 2024 and 2025 National Health Interview Survey (NHIS) Sample Adult public-use files from the National Center for Health Statistics (NCHS). The analytic sample includes 6,397 adults with diagnosed diabetes who answered the GLP-1 question.

Methods

Cross-sectional analysis using survey weights, strata, and primary sampling units throughout. Weighted prevalence was estimated with Korn-Graubard 95% confidence intervals. Survey-weighted logistic regression models were adjusted for age, sex, race and ethnicity, education, body mass index category, and urbanicity, with sensitivity analyses using prevalence ratios and alternative covariate sets.

Findings

Weighted 2024 estimates matched published NCHS figures exactly. GLP-1 use was highest among adults with obesity and those aged 50 to 64 years. Lifetime depression was associated with higher crude odds of use, but the association weakened after adjustment, and sociodemographic differences in use were unchanged after accounting for mental health.

FOR THE TECHNICAL READER

Survey years were pooled by stacking files with weights divided by 2. Subgroups were analyzed as domains of the full design. Missing data were handled by complete-case analysis (under 2% missing for every covariate). All main tables were recomputed independently in Python, and an earlier unweighted analysis was audited, reproduced, and corrected. Reporting follows the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines.
