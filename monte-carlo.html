Variance inflation in Monte Carlo simulation.

What happens to the variance of an estimate when a simulation draws data from an estimated parameter instead of the true one?

Research with Xin Tu, PhD, UC San Diego. The starting normal-mean simulation was written by Muhan An; the generalized simulation is by Jade Cruz.

The problem

Simulation-based power analysis and parametric bootstraps usually simulate from a pilot estimate rather than the unknown truth. That adds a second layer of randomness that is rarely accounted for.

The data

Simulated data for a normal mean and a logistic regression slope, at sample sizes from 50 to 1,000, with 4,000 replicates per setting.

Methods

The law of total variance predicts that simulating from an estimate roughly doubles the variance of the resulting estimator. A generalized simulator in R measured this inflation ratio across sample sizes and evaluated plug-in power calculated from simulated pilot studies.

Findings

For the normal mean, the inflation ratio matched the theoretical value of 2 at every sample size. For the logistic regression slope, the ratio rose above 2 in small samples (2.49 at n = 50). Plug-in power was highly unstable: with a pilot of 40 per group and a true power of 0.56, 47% of pilots gave estimated power below 0.5 and 24% gave estimated power above 0.9.

FOR THE TECHNICAL READER

The Monte Carlo standard error of each ratio is about 0.04 to 0.09. The small-sample excess for the logistic slope is consistent with a sampling variance that depends on the parameter, together with small-sample bias away from zero and heavy tails.

Next steps

Derive the small-sample excess analytically for generalized linear models, test corrected estimators, extend the study to Poisson, Cox, and mixed models, and develop guidance on pilot study size.
