iModulon transcriptomics of A. baumannii.

What regulatory programs drive gene expression in the multidrug-resistant pathogen Acinetobacter baumannii, and how do they relate to each other?

Course project for Systems Biology & Bioengineering II, UC San Diego (Winter 2026).

The problem

Acinetobacter baumannii is a multidrug-resistant, hospital-acquired pathogen. Knowing which groups of genes switch on and off together can point to how it adapts to stress and survives treatment.

The data

1,498 public RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) samples from 136 independent projects in the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) databases, covering 3,718 genes.

Methods

Independent component analysis (ICA) factored the expression matrix into iModulons: sets of genes that rise and fall together because a shared regulator controls them. Relationships between iModulons were measured at the sample, condition, and within-project levels, with project-level bootstrap confidence intervals. Principal component analysis and k-means clustering described condition structure. 705 experimental conditions were curated for iModulonDB.

Findings

Ribosome and ATP synthase activity rose and fell together (r = 0.71; 95% confidence interval, 0.61 to 0.79), and the relationship went the same direction in 86% of individual studies, matching the known link between growth and energy supply. Arsenic resistance and type IV pili were inversely related (r = -0.32), suggesting a possible stress-versus-motility trade-off, though it was less consistent across studies.

FOR THE TECHNICAL READER

The database was uploaded to iModulonDB and passed its validator with no failed checks. Unlike principal components, independent components are chosen to be statistically independent, which tends to line up with real regulators.

Next steps

Test whether arsenic exposure reduces pili expression and biofilm formation in a controlled experiment.
