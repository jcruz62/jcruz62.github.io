The cost of a nudge.

Can a machine learning policy send fewer walking reminders while keeping their estimated benefit on short-term physical activity?

Course project for Web Mining & Recommender Systems, UC San Diego.

PythonOff-policy evaluationmHealthIn progress
193,723Decision points
294Participants

The problem

Activity reminders in mobile health (mHealth) apps help only some of the time, and too many lead people to tune them out. A policy that sends fewer, better-timed reminders could keep the benefit at a lower cost to the user.

The data

The myBPmyLife micro-randomized trial (294 participants, 193,723 decision points, steps in the 60 minutes after each decision), with HeartSteps V1 as a backup dataset.

Methods

Each reminder is treated as a send-or-stay-silent decision. Because reminders were randomized, the logged send probabilities allow new policies to be evaluated off-policy with inverse probability weighting. An uncertainty-aware policy is compared with practical baselines on held-out participants, with cluster bootstrap confidence intervals.

Findings

In progress. The data audit is complete.

FOR THE TECHNICAL READER

Splits are made by participant so that evaluation is on entirely unseen people. A null result, where no policy beats a simple rule, is treated as a valid outcome.

Next steps

Replicate the published average effect, build the baseline policies, then tune and compare the uncertainty-aware policy at matched notification budgets.
